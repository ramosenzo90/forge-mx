Bolt Check: Points to Hit After Every Ride

Maintenance · 4 min read · Jan 26, 2026

Dirt bikes vibrate, get hammered by impacts, and shake fasteners loose. A two-minute bolt check after every ride catches problems before they cost you a part — or a crash. Here's the walk-around and the spots that loosen first.

Use a Torque Wrench

"Tight" by feel is how bolts get stripped or snapped. Check critical fasteners against the torque specs in your manual. A small torque wrench lives in every good pit box.

The High-Priority Points

  • Axle nuts, front and rear.
  • Sprocket bolts — these back out and can be catastrophic if one drops into the chain.
  • Spoke nipples — tap around the wheel; a loose spoke sounds dull instead of a clean ring.
  • Engine mount bolts.
  • Handlebar clamp and triple clamp bolts.
  • Foot peg mounts and brake/shift levers.
  • Linkage and shock bolts.
  • Subframe and rear fender bolts.
  • Brake caliper and rotor bolts.

Quick Routine

  1. Start at the front wheel and work back, checking each point above.
  2. Spin both wheels and squeeze the brakes to confirm everything's solid.
  3. Note anything that was loose — if a bolt keeps backing out, it may need thread locker or a new fastener.

Little Habit, Big Payoff

Loose bolts rarely fix themselves — they get worse. Building the post-ride bolt check into your routine keeps the bike safe, quiet, and reliable, and it's the easiest way to catch a developing problem early.
